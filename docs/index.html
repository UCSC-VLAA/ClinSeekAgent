Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) and agentic systems have shown promise for clinical decision support, but existing works largely assume that evidence has already been curated and handed to the model. Real-world clinical workflows instead require agents to actively seek, iteratively plan, and synthesize multimodal evidence from heterogeneous sources. In this paper, we introduce ClinSeekAgent, an automated agentic framework for dynamic multimodal evidence seeking that shifts the paradigm from passive evidence consumption to active evidence acquisition. Given a clinical task and access to raw patient data, ClinSeekAgent queries longitudinal EHR records and invokes imaging analysis tools when relevant; refines its hypotheses as new information emerges; and integrates the collected evidence into grounded clinical decisions. We validate ClinSeekAgent in two roles: as an inference-time agent for frontier LLMs and as a training pipeline for open-source models.

For inference-time validation, we construct ClinSeek-Bench, which covers 13 text-only EHR task categories and 6 multimodal EHR–chest X-ray task categories, and pairs each question with both a Curated Input of pre-selected evidence and the raw patient data for automated evidence seeking. Without any benchmark-specific curation, ClinSeekAgent significantly outperforms curated input for both Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol, improving overall F1 from 61.7 to 63.9 and from 57.8 to 59.3 on text-only EHR tasks, and from 66.5 to 68.8 and from 60.0 to 62.5 on multimodal tasks. The largest gains occur in text-based decision-making and multimodal phenotype prediction, and ablations show that they come from multi-round evidence seeking rather than from access to more of the patient record.

For training-time validation, we fine-tune Qwen3.5-35B-A3B on ClinSeekAgent trajectories generated by GPT-5.6 Sol. The resulting ClinSeek-35B-A3B reaches 35.6 average F1 on AgentEHR-Bench (+16.1 over its base model), outperforming all evaluated open-source models, including ones more than ten times larger, as well as Claude Opus 5.

Key findings

What changes when the agent seeks its own evidence instead of receiving a curated package.

(a)

Evidence seeking beats curated input

Without benchmark-specific curation, ClinSeekAgent significantly raises overall F1 for both frontier models on both benchmarks: Claude Opus 5 61.7 → 63.9 (text) and 66.5 → 68.8 (multimodal); GPT-5.6 Sol 57.8 → 59.3 and 60.0 → 62.5. The largest gains are on text decision making and multimodal phenotype prediction.

(b)

Multi-round seeking is the source

Putting the full record in the prompt adds only +0.8 / +1.9 (text) and +0.1 / +0.3 (multimodal) over Curated Input (Opus 5 / Sol), and single-pass retrieval gives no significant gain. Making retrieval iterative adds +5.4 / +1.7 (text) and +6.1 / +1.4 (multimodal) over single-pass retrieval.

(c)

The gain grows with record length

Below 30k tokens of patient record, ClinSeekAgent is on par with both prompt-based settings. On records above 250k tokens, its margin over Curated Input reaches +4.6 (Opus 5) and +5.3 (Sol).

(d)

Distillable into an open model

Fine-tuned on GPT-5.6 Sol trajectories, ClinSeek-35B-A3B reaches 35.6 average F1 on AgentEHR-Bench (+16.1 over its base), outperforming all evaluated open-source models and Claude Opus 5 (31.6), at 92% of its teacher (38.8).

Method: agentic evidence seeking over raw patient data

Each task gives the agent a patient identifier, a prediction time, and an instruction, but not the curated patient context. The agent then runs an open-ended trajectory τ = (x, (ak, ok)k=1..K, ŷ) of tool calls and observations, for up to 200 rounds, before calling finish with its answer. ClinSeekAgent standardizes the environment and tool interface; it imposes no ordering over evidence sources, so the evidence-seeking policy comes from the model.

Temporal policy

Each question gets a question-specific view of the record that keeps only what was available before the prediction time, and every EHR tool (including SQL) and every Curated Input prompt reads from this same view. Diagnosis and procedure codes become available only 24 hours after their encounter ends, and the codes of the encounter being predicted are always hidden. A linked chest X-ray is visible only if acquired before the cutoff, and its report only if finalized by then.

Where does the gain come from?

ClinSeekAgent differs from Curated Input in two ways: it can access the whole record instead of a curated window, and it gathers evidence over multiple rounds of tool calls and reasoning. Two ablation settings extend record access without the multi-round loop; all four settings share the same temporal policy.

Full-Record Input

Curated Input with the patient's full pre-cutoff record in the prompt, truncated oldest-first when it does not fit the 100k-token context window.

Single-Pass Retrieval

ClinSeekAgent with the same tools and record access, but all retrieval calls must be issued in one turn before answering.

  • ▸Extended record access alone does not help. Full-Record Input exceeds Curated Input by only +0.8 / +1.9 on text and +0.1 / +0.3 on multimodal (Opus 5 / Sol); Single-Pass Retrieval shows no significant gain over Curated Input.
  • ▸Multi-round reasoning is the source of the gain. Single-Pass Retrieval and ClinSeekAgent differ only in whether retrieval is iterative; the gap is +5.4 / +1.7 on text and +6.1 / +1.4 on multimodal.

The improvements are stable across runs

Over five runs of GPT-5.6 Sol, the standard deviation of overall F1 is at most 0.4 points within each mode, ClinSeekAgent beats Curated Input in every run, and Krippendorff's α of per-question F1 across runs is 0.87–0.91.

More models

Models with stronger agentic capability benefit more. For Claude Opus 4.6, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6, all three stay within two points of Curated Input overall on text; on multimodal, Claude Opus 4.6 gains +5.1 (mostly on phenotype) and Kimi K2.6 +2.0, and only the gain of Opus 4.6 is significant.

Training-time validation: ClinSeek-35B-A3B

The gain of ClinSeekAgent is consistent only for models with strong agentic capability, so we use it as a training pipeline to transfer this capability to a smaller open model. GPT-5.6 Sol runs ClinSeekAgent on the 3,000 questions of the AgentEHR-Bench training split, and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B is fully fine-tuned on the resulting trajectories, with the teacher's reasoning in the student's native thinking block and tool calls in its native format.

2,628 training / 53 validation trajectories 2× H200 · tensor 2 / expert 2 parallel 52K-token sequences 3 epochs, epoch-2 checkpoint (lowest validation loss) lr 2×10−5, cosine decay

Open-source state of the art on AgentEHR-Bench

ClinSeek-35B-A3B improves on all five tasks, from +7.6 on Microbiology to +23.9 on Labs; it beats Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, with about eleven times more parameters, by +7.5, exceeds Claude Opus 4.6 by +2.4 and Claude Opus 5 by +4.0, and reaches 92% of its teacher (35.6 vs. 38.8), matching it on Transfers (27.4 vs. 27.3). Evaluation uses 100 test questions per task, none of which appears in the training data; all models run ClinSeekAgent with the same tools and temporal policy.

What does ClinSeek-35B-A3B learn?

Over the 500 AgentEHR-Bench trajectories, both precision (18.6 → 32.4) and recall (31.1 → 53.3) improve over the base model, accompanied by two changes in behavior.
